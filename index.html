03 / Engineering Process

How I Approach Engineering Problems

A disciplined six-stage workflow for taking an engineering question from physical principles to verified analytical and experimental conclusions.

Step 01

Problem Definition

Establish system requirements, operational environment, feed pressures, mass flow budgets, and failure thresholds.

Step 02

Assumptions & Bounds

Identify physical constraints, boundary conditions, unchoked/choked regimes, and simplify without losing essential physics.

Step 03

Physical Principles

Ground the model in fundamental governing equations — conservation of mass, momentum, Fourier's law, and Bartz convection.

Step 04

Analytical Modeling

Implement numerical solvers, run NASA CEA combustion simulations, and audit preliminary calculation formulas for errors.

Step 05

Results & Margins

Interpret temperature gradients, Mach profiles, and pressure drops against material softening and acoustic stability margins.

Step 06

Verification & Comms

Document findings in clear engineering presentations, acknowledge limitations, and verify against physical testing.

Data Analysis & Research · WISP

WISP Torch Ignitor — Analytical Overview

A collaborative propulsion engineering project conducted with the Wisconsin Space Program (WISP) at UW–Madison, evaluating an uncooled GOX/propane torch igniter.

Technical Highlights

  • Transient Heat Conduction: Formulated 1D radial heat conduction using Fourier's law and the Bartz equation to compare C110 Copper, 304 Stainless Steel, and 6061-T6 Aluminum. Verified C110 Copper inner wall remains under 640 K during a 6.0 s test burn, comfortably below its 800 K softening limit.
  • Injector Orifice Sizing: Sized compressible orifices for 6.0 g/s GOX (Atot = 6.26 mm2) and 4.0 g/s gaseous propane (Atot = 3.65 mm2) at 20 PSID (ΔP = 22.2% Pc), demonstrating why gaseous operation prevents orifice clogging.
  • Geometric Spreadsheet Audit: Discovered and resolved formula errors in contraction ratio scaling and chamber residence time, expanding volume to 4155.5 mm3 (tres = 0.207 ms, a 7.8x flame stability increase).
  • Shifting Equilibrium Aerodynamics: Synthesized NASA CEARUN simulations showing Mach 1.75 supersonic exit expansion, 7.2 MW/m2 peak convective heat flux, and exhaust mass fractions dominated by CO and H2O.

Transient Nuclear Reactor Xe-135 Simulator

A visual, real-time representation of Xenon-135 behavior in a reactor core during load-following transients.

Technical details

  • Numerical solvers in JavaScript integrate the coupled Iodine-135 and Xenon-135 concentration equations over simulated time.
  • A control interface lets the operator move reactor power and watch the delayed isotopic response that follows.
  • Core dynamics are visualized to illustrate xenon-pit dead time and the risk of spatial power oscillation.
